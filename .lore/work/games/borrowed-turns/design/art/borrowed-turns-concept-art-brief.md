# Borrowed Turns: concept-art exploration brief

## Goal

Explore visual directions for **Borrowed Turns**, a small turn-based tactical game prototype. Treat art exploration like gameplay exploration: propose genuinely different alternatives, compare them fairly, gather Ronald's reactions, and refine what shows promise. Do not settle on a finished art style immediately.

Borrowed Turns is the game's working title. Mind Garage is the broader AI-led game-design experiment, not the game's title. The initial audience is Ronald alone; his feedback is not evidence of general market preferences.

## Game premise

An ordinary person carrying a stolen artifact must escape pursuing enemies in a small grid-based encounter. The central mechanic is stealing an enemy's announced action, then turning that ability against the hunt.

The latest agreed design direction, not yet implemented in the prototype:

- Capturing an ability is free, initially requires close proximity, and has no per-turn capture cap.
- Movement has its own budget and can be interleaved with actions and capture.
- Releasing a captured ability costs an action and uses that ability's range.
- Holding an ability creates pressure to release it before a deadline.
- Replacing a held ability or letting it expire triggers its specific backlash, ideally discharging it where the player stands.
- There is no harmless discard. Backlash might hurt nearby pursuers as well as the player, making deliberate exposure a possible tactic.

Exact timing and budgets remain unsettled. Do not invent new gameplay requirements to support an image.

## Player promise

Ronald wants short, complete runs where choices reshape play and completion opens enticing new ways to approach the next run. New possibilities should feel worth anticipating and trying, not merely increase numbers or change labels.

No setting, art style, 2D/3D approach, or commercial audience has been selected. The prototype uses Godot. Keep eventual production feasibility in view without treating today's placeholder visuals as art direction.

## Exploration

Propose **six genuinely distinct visual directions**, not palette swaps. For each, briefly describe:

- Its visual identity and the experience it might support.
- How the ordinary protagonist, stolen artifact, pursuers, and borrowed power belong together.
- How enemy intentions, capture, held power, release, and backlash could remain distinguishable at gameplay scale.
- Why it might appeal to Ronald, what might fail, and what feedback would challenge the proposal.

Compare equivalent material across directions: the same small encounter and gameplay moment, with a gameplay-scale view rather than only cinematic illustrations. You may propose a neutral comparison scene, but label any invented setting or ability as illustrative, not established game content.

## Feedback and iteration

Decide what each comparison is testing before reviewing the images. Ask separately:

1. Do I like this image?
2. Do I want to play a game that looks like this?
3. Can I understand the threats and what the stolen power is doing?

AI critique can identify possible readability problems; it cannot establish Ronald's taste or enjoyment. Preserve candidates, predictions, feedback, and rejected directions. Refine a small shortlist through bounded comparisons rather than polishing a winner prematurely. Record which ideas came from the agent and which came from Ronald.

## First deliverable and boundaries

Start with concise written proposals and a comparison plan for Ronald's approval before generating images. Ask only questions that block useful exploration; make bounded proposals for the rest.

This brief requests concept exploration, not finished assets, modeling, or game implementation. Obtain explicit approval before image generation or paid rendering. Concept selection does not authorize production assets or integration into the game. No repository access is needed for this first stage; this document supplies the starting context.
