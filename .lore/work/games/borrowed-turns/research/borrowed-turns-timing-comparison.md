# Borrowed Turns timing comparison

## Key Findings

- The shipped Circler opening gives Capture a positional decision before any clock is added. Step west to save 1 HP and forgo the first borrow, or take the shove. Which lane you take it in changes where Circler goes and what you can do with the borrowed shove.
- A matched real-time overlay adds a deadline, not a new way to capture. Pause can buy back thinking time; no pause can reward fast reading and reliable execution. There is no advantage to arming at the last instant versus earlier in the same beat. Don't add a parry window and attribute its effect to the clock.
- If the player has already decided and is waiting on confirmation or animation, test a quicker untimed interface first. Test real time only for a skill Ronald might want to practice, not to make the same inputs happen faster.

## Shared position — verified at `df36cd6`

Open the shipped `circler` encounter on turn 1: 7×7, no walls, player at `[2,3]` with 10 HP, Circler (`A`) at `[4,3]` with 4 HP, 3 AP, nothing armed or held. Its opening intention is visible immediately: a west-facing shove that deals 1 damage and pushes, covering **columns 2–3, rows 2–4**. The objective in all three timelines is still to defeat Circler; compare the opening decision through the first opportunity to use the borrow.

| Commit before the shove | Result of the same enemy response |
| --- | --- |
| **Dodge west** to `[1,3]` (1 AP) | Miss. Player at `[1,3]`, 10 HP, no borrow. If already armed (1 additional AP), arming survives the miss. |
| **Arm and stay** at `[2,3]` (1 AP) | 9 HP, pushed west to `[1,3]`, acquire wide shove. Circler stays at `[4,3]`. |
| **Arm and step north** to `[2,2]` (2 AP total) | Still in the footprint. 9 HP, pushed west to `[1,2]`, acquire wide shove; Circler then moves to `[3,3]` and announces the same shape one column west. With the new 3 AP, move back to `[2,2]` and Shine **at `[2,3]`**, not at the diagonal enemy. The side lane hits Circler at `[3,3]` and pushes it to `[4,3]` at 3 HP. |

Arming is targetless. The next enemy hit you survive applies damage and displacement **before** you acquire the actual attack. It isn't blocked or weakened. An armed miss doesn't spend the arming; a lethal hit never grants a rescue capture. Shine costs 1 AP, can reuse the held attack while its source lives and does not trigger Capture. Circler's opening doesn't re-aim when the player moves. At the enemy boundary it attacks first, then moves, then re-aims and announces; AP resets to 3 (no carryover). A player Shine that displaces the source can translate the new footprint before the next enemy action without re-aiming it.

## Matched storyboards

Use the **arm and step north** branch to show what a successful attempt looks like; the other choices above are legal in every storyboard. `w` is wall time; `g` is proposed combat-clock time. Numbers are examples, not measured play or recommended balance.

| Moment | Untimed turns (shipped rules) | Continuous clock + optional tactical pause (paper) | Continuous clock, no tactical pause (paper) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The warning appears | `w=0`: see all six cells. Waiting, even at 0 AP, does not advance the enemy. | `w=g=0`: see the same cells and a visible 6 s countdown. It starts when the board is readable. | `w=g=0`: same reveal and countdown. |
| Read, arm, get into the side lane | E.g. think for 8 s; choose Capture and Confirm at `w=8` (3→2 AP); choose Move, select `[2,2]`, Confirm at `w=9` (2→1 AP). | Pause at `w=g=2`. Four combat seconds remain; take 8 wall seconds to inspect and make the same two confirmed commands while frozen (3→2→1 AP). Resume at `w=10, g=2`. | Confirm Capture at `w=g=0.8`; confirm the step at `w=g=2.2` (3→2→1 AP). Reading, targeting and confirming all spend time on the same clock. |
| Shove and acquisition | Prepare **End turn**, inspect its exact preview if useful, then Confirm at e.g. `w=10`. Nothing fires until Confirm. | At `w=14, g=6`, the enemy fires automatically. No End turn input; the already confirmed arm counts. | At `w=g=6`, same automatic strike. If the step went through but Capture was only selected, you still take the hit and get pushed; there is **no borrow**. |
| See the consequence and act again | Attack → push → acquire → Circler moves → fresh warning and 3 AP. The model commits in one batch; the scene reveals the new warning after finite feedback. Then move and Shine as above at leisure. | Same batch at `g=6`; the fresh warning starts the next 6 s beat. Choose the move and Shine before `g=12`, or pause to inspect them. | Same next beat starts at `g=6`; move and Shine must commit before `g=12` to affect that upcoming attack. On a timely Shine the warning translates when Circler is pushed, just as it does under turns. |

**Controls in the comparison.** Use the same action selection, tile selection where relevant, separate Confirm and Cancel, visible AP/armed state, footprint and command previews. Arm takes two inputs (select, confirm); Move and Shine take three (select, target, confirm), not counting cursor travel or inspection. Only confirmed player commands spend AP or change the combat state; Cancel only discards the uncommitted selection. Untimed End turn takes two inputs and 0 AP and provides a boundary preview. In the two timed sketches, offer the equivalent noncommitting **“If it hits now”** preview; no early-fire button. At the deadline resolve the enemy batch before any input at exactly that time; discard unfinished selections without AP charge rather than replaying a stale Confirm into the new beat. The clock does not trickle AP back, and spending the last AP never fires the enemy early. Arming is available for the whole beat when AP remains and stays armed across a miss or pause; the **deadline is for this hit**, not an expiry on arming or holding.

**What pause freezes.** One toggle each way, no AP. Freeze the countdown, enemy action/movement and timed warning animation; keep the board, intent, exact previews, pending selection, HP, AP and held/armed state readable. Nothing resets or expires. Confirmed player commands are the only changes allowed while frozen: they apply immediately, one at a time, at the frozen clock value, and spend normal AP. No queue, start-of-beat auto-pause, or free retarget. Without tactical pause, opening Details or hovering a preview never stops the clock. Use static or instantaneous paper feedback in all three conditions so a presentation lock cannot steal time.

These are two **real-time clock** treatments over discrete combat events, not freely interleaving enemy AI. They deliberately keep attack → move → announce/AP refill atomic. Letting Circler orbit between the step and the threatened hit, aim at a different instant, or move again while the player acts could change which landing tile is good. That would test a changed ruleset, not just pacing.

## What changes, and what would contradict the prediction?

| Mode | Choice and skill | Falsifiable prediction for Ronald |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Untimed** | Compare HP, landing tile and next Shine. Decide when to release the enemy response. No necessary reaction speed. | On an unrehearsed attempt he can distinguish the safe west step from the threatened north step and explain why someone might pay the hit. If errors persist with unlimited time and readable information, decision time is not the main barrier; if he reads just as well on the clock, the proposed reading benefit of extra time is unsupported. |
| **Pausable real time** | Same tactical options, plus when to stop the clock; can trade continuity for deliberation. The smallest execution skill is noticing when to pause, not precise capture timing. | In a short comparison he preserves the deliberate choice and can use at most one pause per warning without wanting a freeze at each input. If he repeatedly toggles to handle routine inputs, or consistently pauses immediately and only resumes to wait for the hit, pause preserved control but failed to add the proposed momentum. |
| **Unpausable real time** | Same options remain legal before impact, but a slow decision can close an option. Rewards quick footprint recognition and committing a chosen sequence; targeting, focus and confirmation speed can masquerade as that skill. | Across two matched comparisons, expect at least one more misread or incomplete intended commit without pause than with untimed turns; the clock may favor the familiar single-command arm-and-stay over the two-command flank. Equal or better performance without relying on the memorized opening contradicts this prediction. Evidence *for keeping pressure* would be intact reading plus a specific cue or execution skill he wants to practice, not merely faster resolution. |

**Test slowness separately.** On the untimed rules, sketch an optional direct commit for familiar commands and short/skippable **non-essential** feedback (keep damage → displacement → acquisition intelligible and the exact preview available). Keep End turn player-triggered; don't make it automatic at 0 AP. If that removes the objection without losing deliberate choices, prefer that direction to a real-time change. The timed variants still have forced waiting if the player finishes before the beat ends, especially when repeatedly pausing. If the attraction is timing an interception rather than executing a known plan promptly, a short, clearly cued arming duration would create such a skill, but it changes Capture and needs its own comparison.

## Optional no-build check

With Ronald's consent, use a paper board, AP tokens, and a handheld timer. Give the same rule/preview card and select → target where needed → confirm language in all conditions; don't show the branch results beforehand. Present the same opening once under each mode in shuffled order, stopping after the enemy response and one opportunity to Shine. Note which condition saw the board cold; later attempts aren't independent and can use the learned solution. If needed, repeat on a mechanically checked unfamiliar opening and rotate the order rather than treating a different board as equivalent by assumption.

After each probe, note whether he read the threatening cells, intended to dodge or take the hit, actually committed that plan, and whether a miss was a misread, late input, or chosen dodge. Note pause count and distinguish “I knew what to do” from ability to manipulate the cards. Record enjoyment and desire for another position separately; only count replay if he voluntarily returns. Paper timing and narrator delay cannot measure actual Godot input latency; practice/order, the countdown cue, fixed cadence and removal of the End turn gesture are also limitations. Don't compare full-fight win rates from this slice.

**Stop rule before testing:** if two timed probes produce a wrong threat read or missed intended commit that the corresponding untimed check does not, and Ronald identifies no timing skill he wants to practice, drop that pressure model. Stop immediately if he wants to stop. If both formats produce misreads, investigate display or rules clarity instead. Consider a further pressure test only if he can still explain the HP/position bargain, execute his choice, and wants more of the timing challenge. No build or playtest has been run for the proposed modes.

## Sources and limits

- **Local source:** [encounter registry](../../../../../games/borrowed-turns/content/encounters.gd), [Circler definition](../../../../../games/borrowed-turns/content/opponents.gd), [combat model](../../../../../games/borrowed-turns/model/encounter.gd) (`_apply`, `_end_turn`, `_orbit_enemy`, `_finish_ability`), [selection and feedback controller](../../../../../games/borrowed-turns/ui/encounter_ui.gd) (`_process`, `_choose_action`, `_confirm`). Reproduce the baseline in Godot with `godot --path games/borrowed-turns -- --encounter=circler`. Godot 4.7.2 passed `res://tests/stocked_routes_run.gd` (six routes), `res://tests/stocked_opponents_run.gd` (137 checks) and `res://tests/stocked_scene_run.gd` (188 checks, injected controls) via `godot --headless --path games/borrowed-turns --script <script>`. A temporary five-branch trace using the shipped registry verified the table, plus the flank hit without arming. This validates state transitions and selected UI contracts, not route optimality, physical input latency, or enjoyment.
- [Juul, *Introduction to Game Time* (2004)](https://www.jesperjuul.net/text/timetoplay/): separate a player's elapsed time from time that advances the game's events. Useful vocabulary for asking whether removing waiting would be sufficient; not preference data.
- [Mäkelä & Schmidt, CHI PLAY (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3410404.3414248), [author PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/maekelae2020chiplay/maekelae2020chiplay.pdf): 546 posts from 17 RPG discussion threads. Posters praised both real-time responsiveness and precise control in turn-based/pausable combat; long unskippable animations and repeated pausing were separate complaints. Self-selected discussion of different games cannot isolate timing from controls, parties, or encounter design.
- [Wu et al., *Scientific Reports* (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8904509/): in a within-participant four-armed bandit task, 99 participants under a 400 ms deadline repeated choices more and explored uncertain options less. Reason to watch for a fallback routine, not a prediction of the right deadline or enjoyment in deterministic tactics.
- [Davis, *Into the Breach* design postmortem (GDC 2019), slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/presentations/Into%20the%20Breach%20Postmortem%20Final.pdf) and [transcript, 10–14 min](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/into-the-breach-design-postmortem): its designers tried a real-time countdown and chose reliable telegraphs and deliberate play instead. One game's design history, not an experiment showing that all telegraphed combat should be untimed.
