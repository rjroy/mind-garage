# Borrowed Turns revision 02: executable baseline

This is one revised shared mechanic, not six new variants. Ronald selected free unlimited capture, distinct budgets, range inherited from the captured ability, forced discharge, replacement backlash and no discard. The interpretation below fixes open details provisionally for implementation and controlled tests, not as evidence of enjoyment. `design-revision-02.md`, the original executable rules, original build and evidence remain historical records. The old six-rule engine is superseded in the working game, not maintained in parallel.

## Board and abilities

Keep the original cardinal integer grid, actor collision, walls, fixed stationary enemies, exit, HP, and simultaneous visible enemy intentions. A valid fixture supplies positive board dimensions, walkable distinct actor cells, unique IDs (not `player`), positive HP, cardinal enemy `dir`, and walkable exit. Enemies are ordered by fixture order. The revision has no RNG. Production starts provisionally with 2 action points (AP), 1 movement point (MP), capture range 1. These are fixture data, not assertions about permanent tuning. The test fixture can use 4 MP to exercise Ronald's example.

Each enemy has `ability` data `{kind, range, damage, radius, push}`: `kind` is `bolt`, `shove`, or `burst`; range and damage are positive integers; radius is 0 for bolt/shove and positive for burst; `push` is false for bolt, true for shove, and either for burst. The captured copy retains this entire data and the source's announcement direction, even if the source later dies. Ability properties, rather than a generic fixed penalty, drive both release and backlash. The live baseline has no Invert capability or configuration; the earlier optional preview was explicitly removed by Ronald. Shove pushes normally.

For a line ability, a release target is 1 through ability `range` cells cardinally from player, unobstructed through and including the target; the footprint is the ray from the next cell through that target. For a burst, select a similarly legal target cell, then its footprint is the in-bounds, non-wall cells within Manhattan `radius` of that center, irrespective of intervening walls. This makes the area predictable and bounded without duplicating line-of-sight for each splash cell. Enemy intention uses its fixed `dir`: line footprint extends up to range or the first wall/edge; burst's center is the last reachable ray cell up to range, and its footprint is the same area around it. If no ray cell is reachable, its footprint is empty. Actors do not block rays or blast areas. Footprint cells are ordered nearest-to-farthest for lines and row-major y then x for bursts. Release does not affect the originating player even when a burst overlaps them.

Line backlash hits the player at the origin and then all actors on the stored source-direction ray, up to the ability's range. Burst backlash instead centers the full area on the player; it hits the player and every other actor in its footprint. The stored ability's damage applies to each victim. For a push-capable backlash, the player at the center remains in place; all other surviving victims are pushed one cell away from the origin/center if the destination is free and walkable. A released push moves survivors away from the release origin for a line and away from the target center for a burst; an actor exactly at the burst center cannot be displaced. Each actor is damaged once, then displaced immediately if alive; collect targets from the initial footprint before any damage or displacement, so actors newly moved into it are not added. For line effects, process targets in ray order (the origin player first for backlash); for burst effects process the player first if eligible, then enemies in fixture order. Emit damage then death before any next victim; a player death immediately ends resolution, so later victims are not processed. Remove dead enemies immediately. Displacement cannot enter an occupied cell or wall or leave the board. HP never goes below zero.

## Turn and command order

For off-axis burst displacement, use the cardinal axis with the larger absolute offset from the burst center; horizontal wins ties. This is a provisional deterministic convention, not diagonal movement or a UI-authored calculation.

Start at turn 1 with full fixture-configured AP/MP and all alive enemy announcements. A successful `move` costs 1 MP, moves one cardinal unoccupied walkable cell, and does not resolve enemies. Capture is free and can be interleaved any number of times with moves and releases. It requires a live source with an uncancelled current occurrence within configured Manhattan capture range, irrespective of walls. Each announced occurrence may be captured at most once; captured source occurrences are canceled until the next turn and do not resolve. An empty slot acquires the source's ability immediately. A release costs 1 AP, no MP; it is legal on the same turn as capture, consumes the slot before effects, and uses ability geometry. `discard` and `wait` are not commands. `end_turn` is always available while playing, even with unused budgets. There is no automatic enemy turn when AP or MP runs out, no real-time timer, and no cap on the number of captures.

Replacement first validates the target source and reach against the pre-command state. If valid, the command succeeds and consumes neither budget, then discharges and clears the old slot before attempting the new acquisition. If the player dies, resolution ends with loss: no acquisition or cancellation of the new occurrence. Otherwise, recheck that the original selected source still exists, has its uncancelled occurrence, and is within capture reach of the player's current position. If so, acquire and cancel it; otherwise leave the slot empty and emit `capture_failed` with `source` and reason `source_dead` or `out_of_reach`. The backlash is not rolled back and the new source's occurrence is not canceled. This intentional partial outcome is previewed exactly; it is not an invalid/no-op command. A captured old source may die without invalidating its already stored ability.

An ability captured during turn T has `deadline_turn = T + 1`: release is legal through the entire next player turn. On `end_turn` during T it remains stored. On `end_turn` during T+1, discharge and clear it **before** enemy resolution; death stops resolution. Capture at any time starts a fresh deadline. On every `end_turn`, after any due discharge, surviving noncancelled enemies resolve in fixture order; stop on player death, skipping enemies killed by earlier effects. Then increment turn, refresh both budgets, clear cancellations, and announce surviving enemies' new occurrences. No enemy resolves between player commands. On any successful command, immediately after its player-side effects, if the living player is on the exit and the current turn is at least `opens`, win before enemy resolution. Following `end_turn` enemy resolution and turn increment, check the same condition again, allowing an occupied exit to open. Failure always takes precedence. Win/loss freezes further commands.

## Model and UI contract

Retain the existing `RefCounted` entry point `res://model/encounter.gd`: `configure(fixture: Dictionary, variant: String)`, `reset()`, `snapshot() -> Dictionary`, `assess(action: Dictionary) -> {legal:bool, reason:String, preview:Dictionary}`, `act(action: Dictionary) -> bool`. Revision baseline uses the explicit ID `R2`; this is an identity for the revised baseline, not selection of an original variant. Unsupported IDs are not a way to run the old engine. `configure` deep copies fixture; reset restores it and mode, including fresh budgets. Snapshots and previews are detached JSON-friendly data. Illegal/malformed commands return false or a nonempty rejection reason and `{}` preview without changing *anything*, including events or budgets. For a legal command, `assess` never mutates and `preview` is exactly the full snapshot after `act` with no intervening command. Do not implement model rules in UI or tests.

Fixture shape: `{width, height, walls, player:{pos,hp}, exit:{pos,opens}, rules:{actions,movement,capture_range}, enemies:[{id,pos,hp,kind,dir,ability}]}`. `kind` duplicates `ability.kind` for compatibility with the original scene's actor display and must agree; `ability` is authoritative. Budgets and capture range are nonnegative integers. Valid commands: `{type:"move",to:[x,y]}`, `{type:"capture",source:id}`, `{type:"release",target:[x,y]}`, `{type:"end_turn"}`. Reject extra keys and malformed values, including any `invert` key whether true or false. No V1–V6-specific keys, approach, target commitment, or discard.

Snapshot has fixture board fields including `rules` (maximum AP/MP and capture range), and `variant`, `turn`, `status` (`playing`, `won`, `lost`), `action_points`, `movement_points`, `cancelled` (source IDs in capture order), `stored` (empty or `{source, ability, dir, captured_turn, deadline_turn}`), `backlash_footprint`, `intentions`, and `events`. There is no inversion configuration field. `deadline_turn` is the last player turn in which release is available; the held action expires when that turn's `end_turn` is submitted. `backlash_footprint` is empty `{}` when no action is held, otherwise `{type:"ray"|"area", cells:Array[[int,int]]}` describing the complete backlash footprint at the player's *current* position, including the player cell, independent of HP and whether an immediate discharge would be lethal. For line backlash it starts with the player cell then its ordered source-direction ray; for burst it is the row-major area. It changes when the player moves and supplies geometry without the UI simulating an early `end_turn`. `intentions` is a full current-telegraph array `{source, kind, cells}` in fixture order for every alive noncancelled source. It is computed independently of a lethal hypothetical action; terminal state has no intentions. `events` contains only the latest successful command's events, initially empty. Model-authored `ray` and `area` events expose complete footprint cells even if death interrupts damage, including backlash, so the UI can show the actual geometry. Preview must expose full replacement outcome, including `capture_failed`, loss or acquired ability, not just geometry; a legal lethal replacement is a legal preview ending in loss, never a rejection.

Ordered event dictionaries use `type` plus these fields: `move {actor,from,to}`, `capture {source,kind}`, `capture_failed {source,reason}`, `release {source,kind,target}`, `backlash {source,kind}`, `ray` or `area {actor,kind,cells}` (`actor` is `player` for release/backlash, otherwise the enemy ID), `damage {actor,amount,hp}`, `displace {actor,from,to}`, `death {actor}`, `turn {number}`, and `win` / `loss`. Emit `release` or `backlash` before footprint; footprint before damage; damage before death and loss; an old-slot backlash finishes before new `capture` or `capture_failed`; `turn` follows enemy resolution and budget refresh. An enemy killed before its turn emits no footprint. On invalid commands, even prior events remain unchanged.

## Evidence boundary

The revised controlled test suite exercises the model with test-owned ability values, no production tuning assumptions. Original prototype and historical evidence remain untouched. The UI designer owns layout/interaction decisions; the model supplies legality, ordered predictions and complete footprints. Any diagnostic or playtest must record build, consent, comparison and findings separately. This specification does not assert GREEN, comparative enjoyment, or a new six-way mechanic exploration.
